/about

I make sure the integration layer is boring, reliable, and invisible — so the AI built on top of it doesn’t have to be.

I’m a Product Manager on IBM’s AI Integration team, working across webMethods Hybrid Integration and API Connect — the platforms enterprises use to connect systems, govern data exchange, and now, wire up AI agents to real operational work.

My lens on this space comes from an MBA at IIM Ahmedabad paired with hands-on product work in enterprise software. I spend most of my time translating between two audiences that rarely speak the same language: architects who live in the weeds of APIs and governance, and leadership chasing the next AI initiative — and making sure neither one ships something the other has to clean up later.

Based in Bengaluru, and usually thinking about the same question from a few different angles: what does an enterprise actually need to be true — technically, operationally, organizationally — before an AI agent can be trusted with real work.

/writing

Ideas I keep coming back to

Notes and posts on integration, platforms, and what “AI-ready” actually requires of an enterprise underneath the surface.

  • #AgenticAI #Integration

    The agent gets the credit. The integration fabric does the work.

    Most of the national and enterprise systems now enabling “AI achievements” — governed data exchanges, secure APIs, shared service fabrics — were built years before this model cycle. The agent is the visible layer on top of infrastructure that already had to earn enterprise trust the hard way.

  • #iPaaS #EnterpriseArchitecture

    Operational efficiency is a feature, not a footnote

    Cleaner interfaces, saner runtime key management, automatic cleanup of stale instances — the unglamorous releases are usually the ones that determine whether a platform team actually wants to keep using your product.

  • #APIManagement #DigitalGovernment

    What public-sector integration gets right

    Systems built for cross-agency data exchange are forced to solve governance, consent, and interoperability before they’re allowed to scale. Enterprises building AI agents are running into the same requirements — just later, and under more pressure.
